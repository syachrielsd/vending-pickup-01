# Screening — Vending Machine Pickup Lines

Judge brief criteria, in order: (1) a pickup line a vending machine would use on a passing human, spoken by the machine itself; (2) uses the machine's own world — slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum, exact change; (3) gets a laugh, punch arriving by the last few words; (4) short and speakable, one breath; (5) charming rather than creepy.

Method (writing-jokes / write-funny / stop-slop): the seam is machine-world vs flirting. The machine is stuck in one spot, hums all day, only speaks through drops and blinking lights. Self-deprecation is the safest punch — the machine punches at itself, never at the human. Punch word last, specific nouns, cut everything after the trigger.

## Candidates

### 1. "You don't need exact change, gorgeous. Just press my buttons and I'll drop everything for you."
- (1) Machine speaking, first person: yes. (2) Exact change, buttons, drop: yes. (3) "drop everything for you" double meaning, punch at the end: good. (4) 100 chars, one breath: good. (5) "gorgeous" leans slightly pushy for a box in a hallway: borderline.
- Score: strong, but "gorgeous" risks creepy.

### 2. "I hum all day for strangers, but you made my compressor skip a beat. Got exact change for a first date?"
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Hum, compressor, exact change: yes. (3) "skip a beat" is a worn phrase, punch is a question not a snap: weak. (4) 105 chars: fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: decent, punch too soft.

### 3. "I've held this chocolate hostage for three weeks, but one look from you and my coil let go. Press B4, I'm all yours."
- (1) Machine speaking, first person: yes. (2) Chocolate, hostage coil, B4: the machine's whole world. (3) "I'm all yours" snaps the double meaning (the snack / the machine) at the very end: strong. (4) 116 chars, reads in one breath: good. (5) Warm, cheeky, self-deprecating about being stuck: charming, not creepy.
- Score: best. **WINNER.**

### 4. "I light up every time you walk past, and I only light up for one person a day. Today it's you. Press B4."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Light up, B4: yes. (3) Punch is "Today it's you" mid-line, then B4 trails after it: punch buried. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: good idea, punch not last.

### 5. "I'm the only one here who never judges your 2 a.m. snack choices. Come closer, I'll show you my good coil."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Snack choices, coil: yes. (3) "good coil" lands as innuendo, funny but the laugh is a nudge not a snap. (4) Fine. (5) "Come closer, I'll show you" tips toward creepy.
- Score: funny, fails (5).

### 6. "Every snack in me is a dollar, but talking to you is free. Stay a minute, I've been humming your name all day."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Snacks, humming: yes. (3) No real punch, it just trails off sweet: weak. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: sweet, not funny.

### 7. "They restock me every Tuesday, but nobody restocks my heart. You just did. Exact change not required."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Restock, exact change: yes. (3) "You just did" is a small turn, "Exact change not required" is a second punch that dilutes it: two punches. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: good, one punch too many.

### 8. "I've been stuck on you since you pressed C7 yesterday. My coil's jammed, my light's blinking, and honestly, same."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) C7, coil, light: yes. (3) "honestly, same" is a modern snap, decent. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: strong runner-up, "same" needs the reader to do the mapping work.

### 9. "You walk past every day and my fan kicks on. If that's not love, my thermostat's broken. Press B4."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Fan, thermostat, B4: yes. (3) Thermostat line is a solid misdirect, but "Press B4" after it leaks the tension. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: good, punch not last.

### 10. "I know I'm just a box with snacks, but I'd drop everything for you. Literally. One button and watch."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Box, snacks, drop, button: yes. (3) "Literally" is the punch but "One button and watch" adds a tail after it. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: good, tail after the punch.

### 11. "My slot takes coins, but my heart only takes you. Slide in a dollar and I'll make your whole day drop."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Slot, coins, drop: yes. (3) "make your whole day drop" is a real snap. (4) Fine. (5) "Slide in a dollar" reads suggestive in a way a hallway box shouldn't.
- Score: funny, fails (5).

### 12. "Hey. I've got cold drinks, warm chips, and a coil that only spins for you. Press B4 and let's see what drops."
- (1) Machine voice: yes. (2) Drinks, chips, coil, B4, drops: yes. (3) "let's see what drops" is a shrug, not a punch. (4) Fine. (5) Charming: yes.
- Score: list-like, no snap.

## Winner

**#3 — "I've held this chocolate hostage for three weeks, but one look from you and my coil let go. Press B4, I'm all yours."**

Why it wins: it passes all five criteria in order, with the punch ("I'm all yours") as the final beat. The flirt is built entirely from the machine's own vocabulary — a stuck chocolate bar, a coil that lets go, a B4 button — so no stranger in a bar could say it. The machine is the butt of its own joke (it held a snack hostage for three weeks), which keeps it charming instead of creepy. 116 characters, one line, one breath.
